Face value is the default, not a rule. Here is what the same day's price roundup showed across 10 offers:

- **SteamCardDelivery** sold a $5 card at $7.99, about 60% over face, and a $100 card at $119.99, about 20% over.

- **GiftChill** went the other way on its "GLOBAL" cards: $50 for $44.50 and $100 for $87.99, both about 11–12% under face.
- **Gameseal** listed a 10 USD wallet key at $9.46.

Small cards carry the worst markups. Paying $2.99 extra for a $5 card is a rounding error in absolute terms and a 60% premium in percentage terms, and it is the tier where buying at face value is easiest to find.

Then there is the outlier: one aggregator listed a "$30 Steam wallet card" at $10.95. A 63% discount on a prepaid code with no stated reason is not a deal you should chase. It appeared once, in one listing, with nothing to cross-check it against. Codes at that kind of discount are the ones that arrive already redeemed, come from a stolen card, or never arrive at all. Skip them.

## Currency is the step people get wrong

Steam's own redemption page says it plainly: "Steam Gift Cards and Wallet Codes will be converted to the currency of your Steam Wallet upon redemption, regardless of where they were purchased."

Read that carefully and it does two things. It means a code bought abroad is not automatically useless. It does not mean any code works on any account. Resellers list cards by the account currency they are meant for, and this listing is explicit about it, tagging each tier as redeemable on USD, MYR, EUR, THB, CLP or IDR accounts and telling buyers to "match your Steam account currency."

The two statements are not in conflict so much as describing different stages. Conversion happens at redemption; the card you buy is still issued for a particular wallet currency. Buying the tier that matches your wallet avoids the whole question. If your wallet runs in USD, buy a USD card. If it runs in EUR, the 2 × €20 and 5 × €20 bundles are the ones aimed at you.

## Digital card or physical card: the friend-list catch

Both are worth the same once redeemed. Steam describes gift cards as working "just like gift certificates," spendable on games, software, hardware and anything else sold on Steam. That is the part people misread: you are not buying a game, you are buying wallet balance, and the recipient decides what it becomes.

The difference is delivery. A physical card is a code you can hand over, and it can be redeemed whenever the recipient feels like it. A digital card is sent through your Steam account straight into a friend's or family member's wallet.

That send flow has a restriction that catches people mid-purchase: you pick the recipient from your Steam friends list. No friendship, no send. A 2019 walkthrough of the process spells this out, and while that guide's denomination table ($20/$30/$50/$100 physical, $5/$10/$25/$50/$100 digital) is seven years stale, the friend requirement is the bit still worth knowing before you pay. If the person you are buying for is not on your friends list, add them first, or buy a code you can forward yourself.

The other practical difference: email-delivered codes arrive immediately, both in your inbox and on screen after checkout, so there is no shipping wait and no card to lose.

## Redeeming, and the mistakes that cost money

1. Log in to the Steam account you want the balance on. The redemption page will not accept a code otherwise.
2. Enter the code exactly as delivered, including any groups of characters.
3. Confirm. The value converts into your wallet currency and shows up as balance.
4. Spend it on anything in the store: games, DLC, software, hardware.

The mistakes are predictable. Redeeming on the wrong account is the big one, since wallet balance is tied to the account, not to the code holder. Buying the wrong currency tier is the second. Copying a code with a stray space is the third, and it produces an "invalid" error that makes people think they were scammed when they were not.

One more thing worth saying out loud: a Steam gift card is not a way to buy a specific game for someone at a discount. Steam sales discount games, not wallet codes. The card is worth exactly its balance whenever you redeem it, sale or no sale.

## FAQ

**Can I use a card bought in another region?**
Steam says codes convert to your wallet currency at redemption regardless of where they were bought. Resellers still sell by account currency. The safe move is to buy the tier that matches your wallet, and treat anything else as a question for support before you pay.

**Can I send a digital card to someone who isn't my friend?**
Not through Steam's gifting flow, which picks recipients from your friends list. Add them first, or buy a code and pass it along yourself.

**Does the balance expire?**
The official Steam pages I checked do not state an expiry, and I will not guess at one. If you are buying several cards to stockpile, ask Steam support or the seller before you commit.

**What if I overpay or buy the wrong tier?**
Nothing in the pages reviewed here documents a refund or exchange policy for codes, and once a code is visible it is generally treated as delivered. Check the currency and the amount in the cart before paying, because after that you are relying on the seller's goodwill.
